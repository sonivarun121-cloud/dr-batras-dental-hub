How to Judge Any Dental Clinic — Including Ours

Rather than simply asking you to take our word for it, here is what we think you should check anywhere you consider going. We are comfortable being measured against it.

  1. Check the qualification for specialist work. BDS is the primary dental degree; MDS is a three-year postgraduate specialisation. For implants or orthodontics, ask who is doing the work and what they hold. You can verify registration with the Dental Council of India.
  2. Ask to see the sterilisation area. The best question almost nobody asks. Look for a Class B vacuum autoclave, sealed pouches with indicator strips, and pouches opened in front of you.
  3. Insist on seeing your own X-rays. A good clinician shows you the evidence on screen and explains what happens if you treat it — and if you don't.
  4. Take second opinions for major work. Multiple implants, full-mouth rehabilitation, or extraction of teeth that might be saved. No ethical dentist will be offended, and you are entitled to copies of your X-rays.
  5. Get costs in writing before starting. Including what materials are used and what happens if something fails.
  6. Pick somewhere you will actually keep going. Proximity is one of the strongest predictors of whether people attend regular check-ups — and attendance matters more to long-term oral health than almost anything else.

If you would like to look around before committing to anything, you are welcome to visit — including the sterilisation room. Get in touch or see all treatments.

2. What is the difference between a BDS and an MDS dentist?

BDS is the primary four-year dental degree qualifying a general dentist. MDS is a three-year postgraduate specialisation in a specific field. For complex work such as implants or orthodontics, MDS-level training is significant.
